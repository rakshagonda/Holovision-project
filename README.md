HOLOVISION

HoloVision is an adaptive vision-assistance web appliaction. It uses the camera on a phone or laptop, with AI object detection, to understand what is ahead of the user. It then changes how it helps depending on how much the user can see: a clearer picture, a glowing edge view, or a radar with spoken and audio guidance. It also gives an early STOP warning when something is too close.

Problem statement

Vision loss is not all or nothing. Some people see everything blurred, some see only shapes or bright edges, and some have no usable sight. Each needs different help.
Many assistive tools are built for only one of these groups, or need dedicated hardware. A magnifier helps mild loss but not severe loss, and audio-only tools ignore the sight a person still has.
Spotting obstacles such as a person, chair or bike is hard with reduced vision, and warnings are often in English only, even though many users speak Hindi, Kannada or another regional language.

How we overcome it

One web application for every level of vision. The user picks Mild, Moderate or Severe, and the app changes its output to match.
Mild: a clearer real image. Better contrast and gentle sharpening help people who can still see the whole scene.
Moderate: a bold neon edge view. It shows the outlines of objects and danger zones for people with strongly reduced vision.
Severe: a radar with sound. Left and right beeps and a spoken voice help people who can’t rely on sight.
AI finds and names obstacles. The app recognises people, chairs, vehicles and other everyday objects, and says what and where they are.
Early STOP alert at 100 cm. A siren, a flashing screen, vibration and a spoken warning fire before a collision, and a caution alert comes earlier.
Voice in English, Hindi and Kannada. Warnings and object names are spoken in the user’s own language.
No special hardware. It runs in the browser on an ordinary phone or laptop, with nothing to install.
Privacy by design. All processing happens on the device, and no video is uploaded or stored.
A clear path to better hardware. The same software can later move to a native app with depth sensors, then to smart glasses.
